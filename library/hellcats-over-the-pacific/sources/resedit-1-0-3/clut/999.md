## What this reveals about Hellcats

The palette has several characteristics worth preserving.

1\. The colors are not arranged as a conventional grayscale ramp. Indices 0–3 are white, cyan, magenta and blue; indices 4–9 form a grayscale range; index 10 is dark green; index 11 is near-black; and indices 12–15 are yellow, green, red and black.

This means that palette indices must be preserved exactly. Reordering the colors would change the appearance of indexed image data referring to this table.

2\. The dark green `#217B31` is distinctive. It could be relevant to instrument displays, terrain or interface artwork, but the resource itself does not establish its use.

3\. The grayscale entries are useful for preserving original Macintosh-style shading. Replacing them with evenly spaced modern grayscale colors would change the appearance of assets that reference them.

### Implications for the strict Godot port

For your original-faithful Godot reproduction, preserve `clut` 999 as an independent indexed palette.

The important rules are to retain the exact index-to-RGB mapping, avoid automatic palette reordering, and use nearest-neighbor filtering when displaying pixel art that must remain crisp.

If the original graphics are indexed, convert each pixel's palette index using the corresponding entry above. If you already have pixel-perfect image exports from ResEdit, keep those original exports and use this palette primarily to verify or reconstruct indexed assets.

Recommended next investigation: Identify the `PICT`, `PixMap` or other indexed image resources that reference `clut` 999. That will establish which graphical elements actually use this palette, rather than assigning it speculatively to the cockpit, terrain or menus.
